MYNTIX CUSTOMER WALKTHROUGH GUIDE
=================================

Purpose
-------
Use this guide for a 15-20 minute introduction to Myntix with a school leader,
teacher, or business manager. Keep the conversation focused on their current
process and show only the parts that address their needs. Ask questions and let
the customer do some of the talking; this should feel like a conversation, not
a feature recital.


BEFORE THE MEETING
------------------

[ ] Use a demo organisation with realistic, fictional data only.
[ ] Confirm the demo login works and the right roles are available.
[ ] Have one admin, one staff member, and one student account ready if possible.
[ ] Prepare a few groups, timetable entries, students, rewards, and transactions.
[ ] Confirm the demo organisation name and logo look presentable.
[ ] Check the app on the device and browser you will use for the walkthrough.
[ ] Avoid showing real student or staff information.
[ ] Know which features are live, which require setup, and which are planned.
[ ] Ask permission before recording or taking notes about the customer's setup.

Do not promise integrations, security certifications, service levels, or
features that have not been confirmed. If a question needs checking, write it
down and follow up with an accurate answer.


1. OPEN AND DISCOVER (2-3 MINUTES)
----------------------------------

Say:
"Thanks for making time. I’ll give you a quick look at how Myntix helps a
school run a simple internal credits system: staff can recognise students,
students can track and spend credits, and administrators can manage the rules
and oversight. Before I show it, could I ask how you currently recognise
positive contributions or manage reward points?"

Ask one or two questions:
- "What takes the most effort in your current process?"
- "Who would use a system like this: classroom teachers, wellbeing staff,
  administrators, students?"
- "How do students currently redeem rewards, if they can?"
- "What would make this useful and safe for your school?"

Reflect back what you heard:
"It sounds like the main thing you want to improve is [their stated need]. I’ll
focus the demo on that and show where the relevant controls sit."


2. SHOW THE STAFF EXPERIENCE (4-5 MINUTES)
------------------------------------------

Open the staff credit-management view. Explain that staff can find a student
or group, see balances, and make an adjustment with a reason. Use fictional
demo data.

Suggested narration:
"The aim here is to make the everyday action quick. A staff member finds the
student or class, chooses an increase or decrease, enters an amount and reason,
then confirms it. The reason becomes part of the transaction history, so the
student and authorised staff can understand what changed."

Demonstrate:
- Finding an individual student and viewing their current balance.
- Issuing a small credit with a clear reason.
- If useful, showing the group workflow and explaining how it applies to the
  selected students.
- Where configured, point out transaction limits and the visible feedback if
  an adjustment would exceed the organisation's rules.
- The teacher timetable, if relevant, and how selecting a class can take staff
  to the relevant students.

Ask:
"Would this fit the way your staff recognise students, or would your team need
a different workflow?"


3. SHOW THE STUDENT EXPERIENCE (3-4 MINUTES)
-------------------------------------------

Switch to the student demo account. Show the dashboard and explain it from the
student's point of view.

Suggested narration:
"Students can see their balance, recent activity, and how their credits are
changing over time. They can also browse the school's rewards and make a
request using their balance. The goal is for the system to feel clear and
encouraging, rather than like a spreadsheet students never want to open."

Demonstrate:
- Balance and recent transaction history.
- A simple weekly activity view, if available in this demo.
- Rewards catalogue, item details, and adding an item to the cart/request flow.
- Any student personalisation options that are enabled for this organisation.

Avoid implying that credits have cash value. Explain that the school defines
what credits mean and what rewards are available.

Ask:
"Would students at your school find these rewards motivating? What would they
actually want to earn?"


4. SHOW REWARD MANAGEMENT AND OVERSIGHT (2-3 MINUTES)
----------------------------------------------------

Return to a staff or admin account. Show the reward request queue and the
administrative controls that are relevant to the customer.

Suggested narration:
"When a student requests a reward, staff can review the request and approve
or deny it. The school remains in control of its catalogue, quantities, and
rules. This keeps the student experience engaging while giving staff a clear
way to manage fulfilment."

Show only the settings that answer the customer's questions, such as:
- Currency rules and optional transaction limits.
- Reward setup and stock/quantity rules.
- Groups, users, timetable, or imports if these matter to their operations.
- Organisation branding and appearance settings, if relevant.
- Audit history for understanding important changes.

Do not spend time touring every admin page. Ask which controls they would need
to own and which they would expect staff to manage.


5. DATA, SETUP, AND FIT (2-3 MINUTES)
------------------------------------

Say:
"Every school has a different setup, so the next step would be to understand
your user and class data, who should have each role, and what your reward
process looks like. We can then confirm the appropriate setup and any
integration requirements before discussing a rollout."

Discuss only what is relevant:
- User and group setup, including supported import or sync options.
- Authentication/SSO requirements and whether the school's identity provider
  is supported for the intended setup.
- Roles and permissions: who administers the organisation, who issues
  credits, who manages rewards, and what students can see.
- Data handling, hosting, backups, and support questions. Give verified
  answers only; capture anything that needs technical or policy confirmation.
- A small pilot with a defined cohort, success criteria, and review date.

Useful pilot measures might include staff adoption, time required for common
actions, student participation, reward requests, and feedback from staff and
students. Agree measures with the school rather than assuming what success
means to them.


6. CLOSE WITH A CLEAR NEXT STEP (1-2 MINUTES)
--------------------------------------------

Summarise:
"From what you’ve shared, the most relevant parts are [need one], [need two],
and [need three]. The main questions to resolve before a pilot are [open item
one] and [open item two]."

Offer a concrete next step, for example:
- A follow-up focused on their identity, data, and rollout requirements.
- A limited pilot with named stakeholders and agreed success criteria.
- A technical review for integrations, security, and data-handling questions.

Ask:
"Would it be useful to set up that next conversation with the person who owns
[relevant area]?"

Record after the meeting:
- Their current process and main pain points.
- Who would use Myntix and who would approve a pilot.
- Required integrations, identity provider, and data constraints.
- Questions you promised to verify.
- Agreed next step, owner, and date.


QUICK DEMO CHECKLIST
--------------------

1. Understand their current process.
2. Show a staff member issuing a reasoned credit.
3. Show what the student sees and how a reward request works.
4. Show the review and admin controls relevant to their needs.
5. Discuss setup, data, and unanswered requirements honestly.
6. Agree one specific next step.


PRESENTATION TIPS
-----------------

- Keep the demo to the customer's workflow, not a tour of every feature.
- Use realistic fictional examples and avoid sensitive real-world data.
- Let the customer interrupt and ask questions; adapt the order as needed.
- Use plain language. Explain "credits" as a school-defined internal system.
- Be clear about what is available now versus what needs configuration or
  further verification.
- If the app behaves unexpectedly, narrate calmly, note the issue, and follow
  up rather than improvising a promise.
- End on the customer's likely first use case, not on a long list of features.
